## TUESDAY - Educational Carousel: Deep Dive on ONE Skill

**Format**: 4-Slide Carousel

**Topic**: Rotate between skills from Saturday's post or introduce a complementary skill

**Example: "The Anatomy of a Powerful Question"**

### Slide 1: Hook
**Header**: You're Asking Questions Wrong

**Body**:
Most salespeople ask questions to fill the conversation.

Great salespeople ask questions to create clarity.

Here's the difference ↓

---

### Slide 2: Bad vs. Good Example
**Header**: Weak Question vs. Power Question

**Bad**: "What are your goals for this quarter?"
(Too broad, creates generic answers)

**Good**: "What's the ONE metric that, if it improved, would change everything for you this quarter?"
(Specific, creates real talk)

---

### Slide 3: The Framework
**Header**: The Power Question Formula

1. **Make it singular** ("the one thing" not "things")
2. **Make it personal** (use "you" language)
3. **Make it consequential** (tie to real outcome)
4. **Make it uncomfortable** (slightly)

Questions that create comfort create generic answers.

---

### Slide 4: Your Practice
**Header**: This Week's Practice

Ask this question in your next 3 conversations:

**"What's the one problem that, if it doesn't get solved in the next 90 days, will actually cost you money or opportunity?"**

Then—count to 5. Let them think.

Watch what happens.

**CTA**: Save this. Use it this week. Tag me in your wins.

Most people ask surface questions → get surface answers → wonder why prospects don't buy

This week: ask better questions.

(This is SEEDS - Empathy in action. When you ask questions that hit the real issue, people feel understood. When people feel understood, they buy.)

## WEDNESDAY - VIDEO: Mid-Week Story/Case Study

**Format**: Video Postcard (60-90 seconds)

**Hook**:
"I just got off a call with a client and I have to tell you about this—"

That's it. That's the whole shift.

Yesterday they sent me a text: Three yeses in one week. Not because their offer changed. Not because they got better leads. Because they gave people a DECISION, not a maybe.

This is what I'm talking about with the SEEDS framework—Story matters. But so does structure. You need both.

If you're getting a lot of 'I'll think about it'—you're probably not giving them a clear decision to make. Fix that this week."

**Caption**:
Mid-week client win that I had to share 🌱

The shift: Stop leaving conversations open-ended.
The result: 3 yeses in one week.

Here's the exact framework my client used:
"Based on what you've shared, I see two options—we can [A] or [B]. Which makes more sense for where you're at?"

This is SEEDS - **Story + Structure**.
You need empathy to understand their situation.
